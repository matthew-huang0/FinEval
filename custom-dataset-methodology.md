# Custom Dataset Methodology

This document describes how the custom SEC EDGAR 10-K dataset was
built, verified, and used, and the design decisions behind it.

## Purpose

The three academic datasets (Financial PhraseBank, FinQA, ConvFinQA)
are useful but public and widely benchmarked. The custom dataset was
built to test the models on data they could not have memorized, drawn
from real primary sources, to see whether the findings from the
academic datasets hold on novel data. It also demonstrates that
FinEval can be pointed at proprietary or freshly sourced data rather
than only pre-existing benchmarks.

## Source Data

The dataset is built from the fiscal year 2023 annual reports (Form
10-K) of nine large public companies, sourced from SEC EDGAR. The
companies span six industries to test cross-industry financial
reasoning rather than a single sector:

- Technology: Apple, Microsoft
- E-commerce and cloud: Amazon
- Automotive and energy: Tesla
- Consumer goods: Coca-Cola, Nike
- Banking: JPMorgan Chase
- Retail: Walmart
- Oil and gas: ExxonMobil

For each company, a set of verified figures was recorded from the
income statement, including total revenue, net income, operating
income, gross profit or gross margin, research and development
expense where applicable, segment or product-line revenue where
applicable, and the prior fiscal year's revenue, net income, and
operating income for growth calculations.

## Question Generation

The questions are FinQA-style numerical reasoning questions. Each item
provides the company's financial figures as context and asks a single
numerical question. There are two kinds of questions:

- Direct lookups, such as "What was the company's net income?"
- Calculations, such as margins, segment shares, and year-over-year
  growth rates, expressed as decimals.

A critical design decision is that all answers are computed in Python
directly from the verified figures, never generated by an AI. The
question wording is templated, and the arithmetic is done by code. This
means every answer is correct by construction, traceable to the source
figures, and free from the risk of an AI hallucinating a number. The
dataset totals 91 questions across the nine companies.

## Use of AI

AI assistance was used to help identify and phrase the question
templates and to help locate figures in the filings. It was not used
to compute any answer or to generate any financial figure. This
approach was disclosed to and approved by the project manager before
the dataset was built. The manual fallback, building every question
by hand, was offered as an alternative.

## Verification

Every figure used in the dataset was verified against the original SEC
10-K filing or the company's official financial statements. The
verification covered revenue, net income, and operating income for all
nine companies, plus segment and prior-year figures.

The verification found one error. Nike's operating income had been
recorded as 5,920 million dollars; the correct figure from Nike's
fiscal 2023 income statement (gross profit of 22,292 million minus
total selling and administrative expense of 16,377 million) is 5,915
million dollars. This was corrected and the dataset regenerated. All
other figures across all nine companies matched the filings exactly.

This verification is a strength of the dataset. Because the figures
are confirmed against primary sources and the answers are computed
from those figures, the dataset's ground truth is highly reliable.

## Prompt Consistency

The custom dataset uses the same numerical prompt template as FinQA,
so the two single-shot numerical datasets are directly comparable. The
context includes both the current year figures and the prior year
figures needed for growth questions, so every question is answerable
from the provided context alone, without the model needing to recall
any figure from memory.

## Results

On the 91-question custom dataset, the models scored:

- gpt-oss-120b (large): 90.1 percent
- gemma-4-31b (medium): 98.9 percent
- llama3.2:1b (small): 45.1 percent

These results reproduce the central findings from the academic
datasets. The medium model matches or exceeds the large model, and the
small model performs far worse on numerical reasoning. That the custom
dataset independently reproduces these patterns on novel primary-source
data strengthens confidence that they are real properties of the models
rather than artifacts of any single dataset.

## Limitations

The custom dataset is templated, so its questions are more structured
and uniform than the messy, real-world questions in FinQA. This makes
it somewhat easier, which is reflected in the higher scores. It tests
whether models can extract and compute from clearly presented financial
data, not whether they can parse ambiguous or poorly structured
questions. It is best understood as a controlled, verifiable complement
to the academic datasets rather than a replacement for them.
